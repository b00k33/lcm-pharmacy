Fang Bing Ren questions for the patient

How few questions, which ones, what the patient sees, what you see. Figures come from your own weights for all 25 families.

1 · How many questions?

The 25 families make 300 pairs (Chai Hu or Xiao Chai Hu, Da Huang or Da Cheng Qi, and so on). I picked questions one at a time, each time the one that tells the most still-unseparated pairs apart. Around 10 questions is enough to tell nearly every pair apart; after that each extra question adds almost nothing.

Height of the line = share of the 300 family pairs the answers can tell apart.

What this will and will not do

It narrows, it does not decide. Ten answers move the families into a rough order and fill in the ticks you would otherwise ask for; the examination (tongue, pulse, belly) and Quick assess still do the rest. The ranking above assumes a patient who answers the way your tables expect, and real patients are less tidy, so treat "around 10" as a good starting length, not a promise. If a question turns out to be unclear to patients, its wording is the thing to fix, and nothing about the signs changes.
