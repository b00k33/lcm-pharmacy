When the same family is best on both sides it says Bing and Ren agree. With only one side ticked it says so. Tiles on the family cards now show the fit (45%) instead of raw points; the points are in the hover (Bing 37 of 41, Ren 0 of 70).

2 · Who leads when the ticks are random

3,000 tries, 8 random Bing signs + 8 random Ren signs each, no real patient. A fair ranking should not favour any family: an even spread would be 5% each. Before, the three families with the longest Bing lists won 38% of all tries; the shortest lists almost never led.

Before (Bing first, raw points)After (Bing % + Ren %)
The spread narrows from 1–18% to 2–8%, but it is not flat: a family with a short list swings more on a handful of ticks, so Yin Chen Hao, Fu Zi, Shi Gao and Yu Ping Feng still lead a little more often than 5%, and Wu Ling San a little less. Not a clinical test, only a fairness check. The tests that matter for a real patient are below.
