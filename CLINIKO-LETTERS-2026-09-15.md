## 1. Post Embryo Transfer advice  (template id 1923627282329503662)

Dear {Patient.FirstName},

Please find below some Chinese medicine advice and considerations for you during your two-week wait. We hope it helps!

Your Embryo Transfer

All over the world, embryos arrive and survive in the uteruses of women who are out in the fields tending crops, lifting heavy loads, standing all day at check-out counters, enjoying business lunches with wine or jumping on and off bicycles and horses. Your embryo is slightly different, however, simply because you have put such a lot into getting this far. For this reason, it's worth considering treating yourself and your embryo gently for the next couple of weeks. You've probably heard the analogy of your embryo being like a raspberry seed in a jam sandwich, and yes it's true, it can't fall out, but still, there are some precautions you might like to think about.

Exercise

Movement is good, but high-impact or tiring exercise or strain on the abdomen and lower back is not. Walk, don't run. Be careful with forward bends, sit-ups, back bends or anything that overly compresses the abdomen or or puts strain lower back. Don't overheat and don't lift heavy weights. (For swimming see COLD below). Try not to get overtired or stand on your feet for long periods.

Bleeding

Some spotting may occur after the transfer procedure, or as a result of medication prescribed at this time. This is usually not of concern but please use pads, not tampons.

Cold

In Chinese medicine, one of the factors facilitating the successful establishment of early pregnancy is a "warm womb". For this reason, we advise against swimming, getting chilled around the lower back or abdomen, and the consumption of icy foods and drinks (at least until your pregnancy is well-established).

Heat

On the flip side it is important not to get overheated, so don't have very hot showers, baths or saunas. It's best not to put very hot heat packs or hot water bottles on your abdomen. Warm ones are OK and a good idea if you feel chilled in the abdomen or lower back.

Fumes

Don't wear perfumed skin or hair products when you have your embryo transferred because embryos are particularly sensitive to volatile fumes. It is a good idea to continue this practice for the following couple of weeks and it is especially important to avoid fumes at this time, so please don't plan to repaint or renovate the house just now. Also avoid all fumes like petrol, nail varnish, hair colour and household cleaning products.

Sex

Most Chinese medicine doctors would caution against sex the first week after a transfer and for some women, even in the early stages of pregnancy.

Food

Have a good variety of well-cooked foods, consumed warm. Eat nourishing plain foods and choose fresh foods in season that are pleasing to the palate and the eye. Make sure to maintain good protein intake. Avoid alcohol and minimise caffeine intake. Avoid green tea, white tea and peppermint tea as these teas are cooling in nature.

Flying

Some women are nervous about flying after transfer or in early pregnancy. However, there is no evidence to date to show any increase in miscarriage or in abnormalities of babies whose mums flew in early pregnancy.

Acupuncture

You may have been having acupuncture to help prepare your uterus before the embryo transfer and also on the day of the transfer to minimise uterine contractions and to aid implantation. We advise two acupuncture treatments during the 10 -12 day wait. Schedule the first appointment between 3-5 days after your transfer and then again at 5-10 days after your transfer. This will not only continue to aid implantation but also to keep the mind calm during what can be an anxious time. Cramping or discomfort in the lower abdomen is not uncommon in the days after transfer and in some women is exacerbated by the progesterone you have been given. Paracetamol or codeine are considered safe to take, but acupuncture will help to relieve this symptom too.

Acupressure

It's a good idea to massage the acupressure point (DU20) on the crown of your head. Take a couple of fingers, and slide them up from the backs of your ears to a depression at the crown. That's the spot! Rub it 3 times a day for 5 minutes each time. This point may help to keep the cervix held high. The right spot should feel a bit achy. Ask your acupuncturist if you need help locating it.

Have fun

If you can, the two-week wait is a great time to do things that bring you joy and laughter. This might be immersing yourself in some of your favourite comedy, silly games, fun and socialising... you name it. Not only are these great stress buffers that will help you mitigate any anxious thoughts, but it also from a Chinese medicine perspective is great for the circulation of Qi (energy) to the uterus. From a TCM perspective joy and laughter circulates energy through the heart and uterus!

Warm regards,

Linh

TCM traditionally advises women to avoid cold drinks, chilled foods, and raw foods during menstruation. The reason? Cold can disrupt the body's internal balance—or homeostasis—by constricting blood vessels and slowing circulation in the uterus. Instead, warm, cooked foods and room-temperature beverages are encouraged to support blood flow and reduce discomfort. It's a small shift that can have a noticeable impact on how your body feels during your cycle.

## 6. FER Luteal Phase 3: CD 16+  (template id 1741005708104566072) — the parts used

You are now in your Luteal Phase, often referred to as the two-week wait (2WW). During this phase, oestrogen and progesterone play key roles in supporting the early stages of embryo development. Our acupuncture session today was carefully timed to encourage implantation and support the natural rise in progesterone, which is essential for maintaining a healthy endometrial lining.

CD16+ Post-Ovulation — The corpus luteum forms and begins producing a surge in progesterone (yang phase). If fertilisation occurs, the embryo travels down the fallopian tube, enters the uterus around day 4 post-ovulation, and typically implants between days 8–10. The endometrium secretes nutrients, and stem cell activity supports the nourishment and development of the embryo.

Basal Body Temperature (BBT) — Your BBT should remain elevated during this phase. Ideal range: 36.5°C – 36.9°C. Fortunately, your chart is tracking well in this area. Any deviations have been addressed in your acupuncture treatment and herbal support.

Iron Intake — If fertilisation has occurred, your daily iron needs increase from 18mg to 27mg to support early pregnancy. Please refer to your Iron Guide Sheet for a list of recommended foods.

Lifestyle Tips — Gentle, relaxing movement is ideal—think walking, stretching, or yin yoga. Avoid intense workouts during this time to allow your body to focus its energy on implantation and hormonal balance.
